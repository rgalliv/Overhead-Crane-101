# OHC — Mexico Jurisdiction Pack

**Course:** OCO301C · Overhead Crane Operator Track
**Client context:** Vestas — factory / workshop bridge cranes and port / staging / logistics cranes
**Purpose:** Give a safety director a defensible mapping from the shared OHC ACS to the Mexican STPS normative framework — without forking twelve modules into a separate "Mexico course."

Same doctrine as the Canada pack: one ACS spine, jurisdiction handled as a branch.

---

## Standing rule (unchanged)

Training establishes the operator's **standard of care**. It does **not** confer qualification. The **controlling entity** (the *patrón*) authorizes the operator. Same Gate Master doctrine as the US and Canada branches — a training vendor cannot issue Mexican competency.

## The edition trap (this one is live, and it bites)

**NOM-006-STPS-2023** — *Almacenamiento y manejo de materiales mediante el uso de maquinaria — Condiciones de seguridad en el trabajo*

- Published **DOF 7 March 2024**
- **In force 3 September 2024** (180 calendar days after publication)
- **Derogates NOM-006-STPS-2014** (*Manejo y almacenamiento de materiales*, DOF 11 September 2014)

Note the title change: the 2023 edition is scoped explicitly to **handling by machinery**, where the 2014 edition covered manual handling as well. Any Vestas Mexico material, contractor pack, or vendor course still citing **NOM-006-STPS-2014 is citing a derogated standard.** This is the same class of defect as the B30.2 edition mismatch already documented in this repo — verify before reuse.

## Jurisdiction branches (OHC-01 Task E)

Mexico is **federal** for labour matters — this is the structural difference from Canada, and learners routinely get it wrong. There is no state-by-state OH&S fork equivalent to provinces or US state plans.

| Branch | Authority | Operator gate | Programme note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MX-FED** | Ley Federal del Trabajo + RFSST + applicable NOMs (STPS) | Trained + employer-authorized worker; **DC-3** issued | The default and, for OH&S, effectively the only branch |
| **MX-IMSS** | IMSS occupational risk regime | — | Incident reporting, risk classification, premium consequence |
| **MX-CONSTRUCTION** | NOM-031-STPS-2011 overlay | Same authorization logic | Port build-out and erection scope |
| **MX-STATE** | State labour authorities | Administrative only | **No separate OH&S standard.** Do not teach a provincial analogue |

**Teaching point (`OHC.01.E.R2`):** the single most common error a US- or Canada-trained operator makes in Mexico is assuming a state-level OH&S code exists. Labour regulation is federal.

## The DC-3 — Mexico's Designation Gate analogue

The Canada branch routes to *employer written authorization*. Mexico has a **statutory, named form** for the same function, which makes the branch cleaner to evidence than either North American peer.

**DC-3 — *Constancia de Competencias o de Habilidades Laborales***

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Statutory basis | **LFT Arts. 153-A** (employer training duty), **153-T**, **153-V** (worker's proof of course completed and passed) |
| Issued by | The *agente capacitador* delivering the training |
| Authentication | **Comisión Mixta de Capacitación, Adiestramiento y Productividad** where the centre has **more than 50 workers**; the **patrón or legal representative** where **50 or fewer** — Art. 24 §II of the Normative Agreement, **DOF 14 June 2013** |
| Contents | Worker data · work centre · training agent · course name, duration and dates · signature of the training agent's responsible party |
| Expiry | **Does not expire** — this is why it cannot stand alone as competency evidence |

### The trap, and why the Gate Master doctrine still holds

A DC-3 **does not expire**. An overhead crane designation **must**. A DC-3 evidences that a course was completed and passed — it is *not* an equipment-scoped operating authorization, and it carries no re-evaluation trigger.

**Therefore the Mexico branch requires both:**
1. The **DC-3** — statutory training record; and
2. The **employer authorization** naming equipment, control modes and environments, with requalification triggers — the same instrument the US and Canada branches use.

Issuing only a DC-3 and treating the operator as authorized is the Mexico-branch failure mode, and it is gated as a Risk element (`OHC.01.E.R3`, reinforced at `OHC.12.C.R5`).

## Language discipline

Same principle the Canada pack applies to Quebec, and it is not cosmetic here.

- Learner-facing Mexico material and the designation certificate ship in **Spanish (Latin American construction register)**.
- Stage-2 already carries EN + es-LA narration and visual text across all 22 modules — that register is the reference. Match it; do not re-translate to a European Spanish register.
- The DC-3 is a **Spanish-language statutory form**. Ship it in Spanish; an English rendering is a courtesy copy, never the record.
- Signals: teach the standard signal set with Spanish command vocabulary. Mixed-language signal calls on a shared bay are a documented incident pattern — gated at OHC-09.
